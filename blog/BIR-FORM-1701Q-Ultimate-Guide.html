1. Meet our freelancer

Imagine you build websites for businesses. You send a client an invoice for ₱130,900. The client withholds ₱6,545 (5%) and deposits only ₱124,355 into your account.

Gross professional fees
₱130,900

Less creditable withholding tax: ₱6,545
Cash paid to you: ₱124,355

For your income tax gross receipts, the relevant amount is generally ₱130,900, assuming this is the proper revenue for the period and there are no genuine returns, allowances, or discounts. The withholding tax is not a price discount; it is a tax credit supported by BIR Form 2307.

“Net of sales returns, allowances and discounts” means adjusting for actual refunds, price allowances and discounts—not subtracting the tax your client withheld. Ordinary business expenses also do not reduce the 8% gross-income base.

2. How the 8% income tax works

For a qualified purely self-employed individual, the annual 8% tax is generally based on gross sales/receipts and other non-operating income in excess of ₱250,000.

Annual income tax = 8% × max(0, annual taxable gross income − ₱250,000)

The ₱250,000 is an annual reduction, not a new ₱250,000 deduction every quarter. Quarterly Form 1701Q recomputes income and tax on a cumulative year-to-date basis. Someone who first registers midyear is not automatically restricted to a prorated ₱125,000 reduction solely because they operated for six months. Mixed-income earners have different rules for the reduction.

Eligibility check: The 8% option has requirements, including the applicable VAT threshold and valid election. Confirm your BIR registration and tax classification before following these examples. Percentage-tax, VAT, or other registration obligations may differ.

3. What each Form 1701Q field means

Background / taxpayer information

On the January 2018 ENCS form, the first part identifies the tax year, quarter, whether the return is amended, attachments, TIN, RDO, taxpayer name and registered contact/business information. It also asks for taxpayer classification, applicable tax code, and the income tax rate or deduction method. Match these details to your Certificate of Registration and current filing application; do not select 8% merely because it produces a lower number.

4. First quarter: a real-number case study

Our fictional freelancer, Mika, works with Harbor Digital and Maple Studio. The following quarter totals are adapted from a real filing example, with all client identities replaced. Monthly breakdowns for Q1 were not supplied, so we use quarter totals rather than inventing transactions.

Q1 fieldAmount
54A — cumulative tax due₱9,064
58A — current-quarter withholding (2307)₱18,165
63A — overpayment−₱9,101
₱9,064 − ₱18,165 = −₱9,101

Under the simplifying assumption of no other income, credits or adjustments, the 8% tax calculation implies Q1 cumulative gross income of ₱363,300: ₱9,064 ÷ 8% + ₱250,000. This is a derived figure, not a confirmed invoice total.

Important: The −₱9,101 is the excess of withholding credits over tax due. It is not an extra payment to enter again in Item 56 of Q2. The original ₱18,165 withholding is carried forward under Item 57.

5. Second quarter: the excess credit is already included

Mika's Q2 Form 2307 withholding totals ₱19,180. On the filed Q2 return, Item 57 is ₱18,165, matching Q1 Item 58 exactly. This means the Q1 withholding was correctly brought forward; the separate ₱9,101 negative balance must not be added to Item 56.

₱39,752 − ₱18,165 − ₱19,180 = ₱2,407

If Mika actually pays ₱2,407, it becomes an actual prior-quarter income tax payment for Q3 Item 56. A negative prior-quarter balance, by contrast, is not such a payment.

Reconciliation warning: Mika reports that filed Q2 Item 51A was ₱746,000. If Item 52A was ₱250,000 and no other adjustments apply, the 8% formula gives ₱39,680, not the reported ₱39,752. That is a ₱72 discrepancy. The filed tax amount mathematically implies cumulative income of ₱746,900, a ₱900 difference. Review the actual Q2 Items 47–54 and any adjustments before concluding that Q2 was fully correct. The withholding-credit carryforward itself reconciles.

9. What happens in the fourth quarter?

There is no separate Q4 Form 1701Q. Eligible purely self-employed taxpayers generally use the annual Form 1701A, which includes the full January–December tax year, quarterly tax payments and creditable withholding. Your Q3 payment (₱16,413 in the provisional case study), if actually paid, is accounted for as a prior tax payment on the annual return.

Usual deadlines

  1. Q1 (January–March): May 15.
  2. Q2 (April–June): August 15.
  3. Q3 (July–September): November 15.
  4. Annual income tax return: April 15 of the following year.

Check current BIR announcements for extensions or deadline adjustments.

10. Common situations

Newly registered in July? Your first applicable 1701Q may be Q3. The ₱250,000 annual reduction is not automatically halved merely because you started midyear; confirm your eligibility and actual income facts.

No withholding from a client? Report the proper gross fees; claim only withholding actually supported by your tax certificates.

Negative payable last quarter? Do not place that negative amount in the “previous quarter payments” field. Carry the underlying prior-quarter withholding in Item 57 and actual tax payments in Item 56, as applicable.

Also earning employee wages? Mixed-income rules differ, especially for the ₱250,000 reduction. Seek guidance specific to your status.

Client gave a real discount? Adjust gross fees for genuine returns, allowances and discounts; withholding tax is not one of these adjustments.

11. Checklist before filing

  1. Confirm taxpayer status, registration, and the 8% election.
  2. Reconcile gross fees with invoices, receipts, and books.
  3. Collect and check Forms 2307 from withholding clients.
  4. Carry forward the prior return's Item 51 to Item 50.
  5. Apply the correct annual reduction without duplicating it.
  6. Distinguish prior actual payments (Item 56) from prior withholding (Item 57).
  7. Enter current-quarter withholding in Item 58.
  8. Review all credits, tax payable, and applicable penalties.
  9. Keep copies of filed returns, filing confirmations and payment records.

11. FAQ: What if I miss the filing deadline?

Example: Suppose the applicable deadline really is October 15 and you file and pay on October 17, two days late. A late return with unpaid income tax may attract a one-time surcharge, daily-accruing interest, and a compromise penalty where applicable. It is not simply a flat fee for being two days late.

ComponentEligible micro/small taxpayerOther taxpayer (ordinary rate)
Surcharge10% of unpaid tax25% of unpaid tax
Interest6% per annum12% per annum
CompromiseVaries by violation and the applicable BIR compromise schedule; confirm with the BIR.

Under Revenue Regulations No. 6-2024, qualifying micro and small taxpayers benefit from reduced civil penalty and interest rates. Confirm your BIR taxpayer classification before using those rates. A higher surcharge can apply to fraud or willful neglect.

Example using a ₱16,413 unpaid income tax balance

Unpaid tax₱16,413.00
10% surcharge₱1,641.30
Interest: ₱16,413 × 6% × 2/365₱5.40
Estimated total before compromise₱18,059.70

This is an illustration only: the actual deadline, days late, taxpayer classification, unpaid balance, and applicable compromise penalty must be confirmed. Even a return with zero tax payable can have a late-filing compromise penalty; zero tax does not automatically mean zero filing penalty.

Where on Form 1701Q? Schedule IV: Item 64 (surcharge), Item 65 (interest), Item 66 (compromise), Item 67 (total penalties), and Item 68 (tax payable plus penalties). Do not confuse a surcharge with the amount of tax due.

Important date clarification: October 15 is hypothetical here. The ordinary Q3 Form 1701Q deadline is November 15, subject to BIR deadline adjustments. Filing Q3 on October 17 would ordinarily be early, not late. Before paying penalties, verify the actual deadline and any extensions.
